Why now

California is rebuilding its networks. That is exactly when this skill is scarce.

CalAIM, Proposition 1 and the Behavioral Health Services Act are remaking who delivers Medi-Cal care and how they’re contracted and credentialed — now, across the state. Plans, counties and CVOs need people who have actually built and credentialed networks at this scale before. I have, repeatedly.

Building and credentialing provider networks is the bottleneck in the Medi-Cal and behavioral-health rebuild — and it is the exact thing I’ve done for twenty years.

About

I build the unglamorous infrastructure behind health care: the contracts, the panels, and the credentialing files that decide whether patients actually have somewhere to go. Over twenty years I’ve done it for health plans and provider organizations across ambulatory surgery, skilled nursing, hospitals, and — the part most people avoid — behavioral health.

What I’m known for is the hard scale. A 15,000-provider behavioral-health panel. 300-plus hospitals contracted at once. The NCQA and URAC audits that keep a delegation agreement standing. It’s detailed, deadline-bound work, and it’s exactly what California’s Medi-Cal and behavioral-health rebuild needs right now.

I work directly with the people on the hook — a plan’s network team, a county’s behavioral-health office, a CVO, a provider group — as an advisor or in an interim seat. No layers, no hand-off.
